Both candidates passed native numerical and execution checks. All five selection objectives were proven optimal: demand distribution, duration balance, movement relationships, family distribution and workload economy. Later placement/muscle polishing was deliberately omitted; the displayed per-session arrangement is not a final workout recommendation.

The cap is causally relevant to Nordic reliance, but removing it alone does not produce the intended balanced result. The uncapped solve supplies the hamstring floor using two direct Nordic sets plus 13 indirect hamstring credits from 26 hip-thrust sets. In the configured library, hip thrusts receive secondary hamstring credit and are externally trunk-supported. The top-priority objective therefore rewards this substitution even though physical workload and family concentration worsen. The remaining selection priorities cannot reverse an already locked higher-priority optimum.

This is a limitation of using the available unsupported-trunk count as the leading demand preference together with fungible muscle credits. It does not establish that the indirect credits equal Nordic training stimulus, that hip thrusts can replace knee-flexion training physiologically, or that either plan fits personal recovery. It does not prove a load-progression limitation caused the original result.
