## Programming demand evidence

The automatic pool is derived from eligible definitions with both family and sourced trunk-support evidence, rather than a fixed list of 29 IDs. After preserving earlier reviewed entries and correcting the exact retained worker pool, the current library supplies 40 eligible automatic candidates. The 29-entry editorial review below is a subset of that search domain; it does not exclude other fully reviewed alternatives.

`unsupported_trunk` is classified for all 29 definitions in the exact retained m[50] qualified pool plus four alternatives: “Present” means the documented standard execution is performed without external trunk support; “absent” means the documented setup uses external support. These facts describe standard setup only. They do not estimate fatigue, spinal load, risk, difficulty, or a person's tolerance. ## Regional anatomy and limits

Existing sourced positive upper-body anatomy entries are preserved. The barbell overhead press now has anterior-deltoid primary and lateral-deltoid secondary attribution from the press-variation EMG study ([PMID 35936912](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35936912/)). A regional label means evidence supports positive activation/participation; omission means unknown. These labels do not change aggregate credits. The regional schema cannot express generic pectoralis-major, hamstring, gluteal or quadriceps attribution at the same granularity, so no such regions were inferred for alternatives. In particular, the dumbbell-fly study measured pectoralis major overall, not clavicular and sternocostal portions separately; those subregion fields were left unknown.

The sources mostly provide acute activation or standard execution, not longitudinal comparisons. The metadata supports candidate grouping and the explicit `unsupported_trunk` count objective; it does not establish that an exercise is appropriate for an individual or that reducing a demand tag improves recovery.
